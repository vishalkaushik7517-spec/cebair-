Timira–01

Classical visual phenomena

Selected descriptions from the Timira discussion in Sushruta Saṃhitā, Uttara Tantra 7.

Uttara Tantra 7/11
ऊर्ध्वं पश्यति नाधस्तात्तृतीयं पटलं गते ।
महान्त्यपि च रूपाणि च्छादितानीव वाससा ॥

ūrdhvaṃ paśyati nādhastāt tṛtīyaṃ paṭalaṃ gate | mahānty api ca rūpāṇi cchāditānīva vāsasā ||

Meaning: The passage describes altered visual perception in relation to direction and an obscured or covered appearance of forms.

Modern phenotype: reduced visual clarity / visual obscuration.

Evidence status: Phenotypic correspondence — not a diagnosis.

Uttara Tantra 7/12
कर्णनासाक्षियुक्तानि विपरीतानि वीक्षते ।
यथादोषं च रज्येत दृष्टिर्दोषे बलीयसि ॥

karṇa-nāsākṣi-yuktāni viparītāni vīkṣate | yathādoṣaṃ ca rajyeta dṛṣṭir doṣe balīyasi ||

Meaning: The text describes altered or abnormal perception and increasing visual disturbance with stronger doṣic involvement.

Modern phenotype: visual distortion or altered perception; differential diagnosis is broad.

Evidence status: Exploratory.

Uttara Tantra 7/13
अधःस्थिते समीपस्थं दूरस्थं चोपरिस्थिते ।
पार्श्वस्थिते तथा दोषे पार्श्वस्थानि न पश्यति ॥

adhaḥsthite samīpasthaṃ dūrasthaṃ coparisthite | pārśvasthite tathā doṣe pārśvasthāni na paśyati ||

Meaning: The described disturbance varies with the location of the defect, including inability to perceive objects in particular spatial regions.

Modern phenotype: possible visual-field disturbance; requires ophthalmic and neurological assessment.

Evidence status: Potential phenotype correspondence.

Uttara Tantra 7/14
समन्ततः स्थिते दोषे सङ्कुलानीव पश्यति ।
दृष्टिमध्यगते दोषे स एकं मन्यते द्विधा ॥

samantataḥ sthite doṣe saṅkulānīva paśyati | dṛṣṭimadhyagate doṣe sa ekaṃ manyate dvidhā ||

Meaning: With the described central visual disturbance, the person perceives one object as two.

Modern phenotype: diplopia-like symptom. Modern assessment should distinguish monocular from binocular diplopia and investigate its cause.

Evidence status: Strong symptom-level correspondence.

Uttara Tantra 7/15
द्विधास्थिते त्रिधा पश्येद्बहुधा चानवस्थिते ।
तिमिराख्यः स वै दोषः ... चतुर्थं पटलं गतः ॥

dvidhāsthite tridhā paśyed bahudhā cānavasthite | timirākhyaḥ sa vai doṣaḥ ... caturthaṃ paṭalaṃ gataḥ ||

Meaning: The passage describes perception of three or multiple forms in the stated circumstances and relates the condition to the fourth paṭala.

Modern phenotype: polyopia-like or multiple-image visual phenomenon.

Evidence status: Symptom-level correspondence.

CEBAIR source-critical note: Sanskrit text, traditional interpretation and modern clinical correlation are deliberately separated. “Timira = myopia,” “Timira = cataract,” or “Timira = diplopia” should not be stated as established equivalences without appropriate clinical evidence.
Research method

Classical text → phenotype → measurement → evidence

01 · Classical source

Record text, chapter, verse, Sanskrit and transliteration.

02 · Phenotype

Convert the clinical description into a clearly defined observable symptom.

03 · Modern differential

List plausible ophthalmological explanations rather than forcing a single diagnosis.

04 · Measurement

Identify tests such as visual acuity, refraction, slit-lamp examination, fundus examination or perimetry where appropriate.

05 · Evidence grading

A: direct validation · B: strong correspondence · C: plausible correlation · D: hypothesis.

06 · Research gap

Turn the classical observation into a testable clinical research question.
